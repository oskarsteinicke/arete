    How it works

    Through the night you go round a cycle of light sleep, deep sleep and dream (REM) sleep, about 90 minutes at a time. Waking at the end of a cycle tends to feel easier than being pulled out of deep sleep, which is where the groggy, heavy feeling comes from.

    So the calculator counts whole cycles back from your alarm, or forward from your bedtime, and adds about 15 minutes to fall asleep. Six cycles is 9 hours, five is 7.5 hours, and four is 6 hours.

    A rule of thumb, not a stopwatch

    Cycles aren't exactly 90 minutes. They differ from person to person and change through the night, with more deep sleep early and more dream sleep towards morning. No calculator can hit the exact minute, so treat the times as a guide.

    How long you sleep matters more than when the alarm goes off. Most adults need at least 7 hours a night. If you're short, an earlier bedtime does more than timing your cycles.

    Sleeping better

    • Wake at the same time every day, weekends included. It anchors everything else.
    • Get daylight soon after waking, and keep the evening dim.
    • Stop caffeine about 8 hours before bed. It lingers longer than it feels like it does.
    • Keep the room cool and dark.

    If you're often exhausted however long you sleep, or someone says you stop breathing in your sleep, see a doctor.
