How it's worked out

Three steps, the same ones the Arete app uses when it sets your targets.

1. Your resting burn (BMR). This is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the one most dietitians start from. It estimates what your body burns lying still for a day.

Men: 10 × weight in kg + 6.25 × height in cm − 5 × age + 5
Women: 10 × weight in kg + 6.25 × height in cm − 5 × age − 161

2. Your daily burn (TDEE). Your resting burn times how active you are. This is maintenance: eat this much and your weight should hold.

Protein stays higher while you lose weight, to help hold on to muscle. Carbs go up when you gain, to fuel training. Many lifters aim for 1.6 to 2.2 g of protein per kg of bodyweight (about 0.7 to 1 g per lb).

Why your real number is different

Any formula is an estimate. Two people with the same height, weight and age can burn a few hundred calories apart. Treat the result as a starting point: eat at it for two or three weeks, watch the trend on the scale, and move it by 100 to 200 calories if your weight goes the wrong way.

A 500-calorie daily gap is roughly 0.5 kg (1 lb) a week. Expect the first week to jump around with water.

When to ask a doctor instead

If you're pregnant or breastfeeding, under 18, have a medical condition, or have a history of disordered eating, talk to a doctor or dietitian before you set a calorie target.
